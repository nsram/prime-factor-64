Practice these strategies in Easy Mode. In Prime Factor 64 Easy Mode, match facts that share a mental move. Larger boards can include two strategies, with a short reminder above the cards. Mistakes here stay in the session and don’t add to Practice Weak Facts. Halve and double is a shortcut you can use across groups, so it doesn’t have a dedicated board.

Halve and double is the first move to practice. Halve one or both even factors to reach a multiplication fact you know. Double the product once for each halving. Then say the full original equation aloud to practice recalling it directly.

The game has eight square facts and 56 other results. Use the four moves below to recover products from familiar facts. Four results also have a second factor pair in the game.

The eight square cards are 11² and 13² through 19². Learn 12² = 144 as an extra anchor. The game uses 144 with 8 × 18 or 9 × 16.

For 17² = 289, remember that 17 is two numbers, 8 and 9. Think “two, eight, nine.” For 19² = 361, think of a 360-degree circle plus one.

For straddles, keep the small squares handy. 1² = 1, 2² = 4, 3² = 9, 4² = 16, 5² = 25, and 6² = 36.

Use each table twice

Cover the result column and give the exact product. Then cover everything except the result and name an eligible factor pair. Start with the pairs that need one doubling. Mix the groups once they feel familiar.

Halve and double

In this deck, halving 12, 14, 16, or 18 gives 6, 7, 8, or 9. If the other factor is already a single digit, leave it alone. If both factors are on that list, halve both. Double the answer once for each factor you halved.

6 × 18 → 6 × 9 = 54 → double to 108

14 × 18 → 7 × 9 = 63 → double to 126, then to 252

Straddle a square

Find the midpoint m and the offset d, which is half the gap between the factors. Square the midpoint and subtract the square of the offset. The midpoint is a whole number when both factors are odd or both are even.

13 × 17 → midpoint 15, offset 2 → 225 − 4 = 221

One square can serve two different moves

Use 14² = 196 to compare the cues. In 13 × 15, the factors straddle 14 by one, so take away 1 and get 195. In 14 × 15, 14 is the smaller factor, so add 14 and get 210.

Build the split at 10

Take the tens part first, then use a small multiplication fact for the remainder. For 7 × 18, think 70 plus eight more sevens. Once 7 × 8 = 56 comes to mind, the whole step is 70 + 56 = 126.

For 12 × 19, start with 120 and reuse 9 × 12 = 108 from the alternate pairs below.

12 × 19 = 120 + 108 = 228

Practice in both directions

Use a square cue in reverse

Given 221, think 225 − 4. Since 225 is 15² and 4 is 2², the midpoint is 15 and the offset is 2. The factors are 13 and 17.

Given 210, think 196 + 14. Since 196 is 14², adding one more 14 gives 14 × 15. For a split fact, practice recalling its pair directly. Use the split to check your answer.

Mixed recall round

Cover the earlier tables. Give an exact product for 1–6. Give one eligible pair from this guide for 7–12, in either order. Try both pairs when the result has two. The answer key follows the round.

Decide what to rehearse next

If the square was slow, review that anchor and its neighboring facts. If the anchor was easy but the product was wrong, repeat the subtraction or addition by itself. If you knew the product from the factors but hesitated in reverse, make the result your next prompt.

To revisit misses from earlier games, open Practice progress in Prime Factor 64 and choose Practice weak facts once the app has a prompt to review.
